If You Only Remember Five Things
  • He invented the slit jet. The 1987 slit pulsed valve (Lovejoy & Nesbitt) is the reason his jets reach 10–30 K with one to two orders of magnitude more cooling collisions than a pinhole source, and why his lines are sub-Doppler. Every radical and ion paper rests on it.
  • He wrote the review that defined a field. High-resolution infrared spectroscopy of weakly bound molecular complexes, Chem. Rev. 88, 843 (1988) — still ~500 citations.
  • He discovered power-law quantum-dot blinking. Kuno, Fromm, Hamann, Gallagher & Nesbitt, JCP 112, 3117 (2000) — 1000+ citations, an entire subfield.
  • He co-wrote the IUPAC definition of the hydrogen bond (2011). He literally helped define the words.
  • He is still breaking new ground. C60 ergodicity breaking in Science (2023); full icosahedral Bose–Einstein statistics in PRResearch (2025); a brand-new home-built OPO in 2025.

How the Machines Work

You will be asked, in some form, "do you understand what we actually do in the lab?" These are the four instruments. Learn the signal path of each one well enough to draw it on a napkin.

1 · The Slit-Jet Sub-Doppler IR Spectrometer — his signature machine

Why a slit instead of a pinhole

  • Density falls as 1/r, not 1/r². A long slit expansion keeps collision density high far downstream, giving 1–2 orders of magnitude more cooling collisions before the free-molecular "freeze-in" region. Result: Trot ≈ 10–30 K and dramatically reduced spectral congestion.
  • Long absorption path. The laser crosses a 4 cm slit instead of a millimetre pinhole, so absorbance scales up directly.
  • Sub-Doppler linewidths. Propagate the laser along the slit, perpendicular to the expansion direction, and the molecular velocity component along the beam nearly vanishes. Doppler broadening collapses to tens of MHz.

Making the unstable species

  • Discharge slit jet: a ~500 V glow discharge struck on the slit jaws cracks precursors into radicals and ions directly in the expansion, then the expansion cools what it made.
  • Chemistry by design: cyclobutyl from cyclobromobutane; phenyl from chlorobenzene; CH2OH by Cl-atom H-abstraction from methanol; ND2H2+ by selective isotopic exchange of ND3 with H2.
  • Concentration modulation: modulate the discharge voltage and demodulate the absorption, so only discharge-produced species survive the lock-in. This is how a radical signal is pulled out from under the precursor.
  • Frequency axis: an ultrastable optical transfer cavity (locked to a polarization-stabilized HeNe) supplies interpolated fringes for absolute calibration.

2 · The Light Sources — he builds them himself

Pb-salt diode lasers

The historic workhorse of the 1980s–90s mid-IR work on Rg–HX complexes. Cryogenic, mode-hoppy, low power — and still good enough to map entire intermolecular potentials.

Difference-frequency generation

Mix two visible/near-IR lasers in a nonlinear crystal; the difference frequency lands in the 3 µm CH/OH-stretch region. Narrow linewidth, poor power. The basis of the classic radical spectra.

The 2025 "JILA OPO"

Home-built cw singly-resonant optical parametric oscillator: a 50 mm periodically-poled lithium niobate fanout crystal in a four-mirror ring cavity, resonant at the signal, pumped by a <20 kHz-linewidth 1064 nm fiber laser up to 15 W. Output: 3–4 µm, >2.5 W, idler tunable 2400–3400 cm⁻¹. Signal locked to a Fabry–Pérot cavity stabilized to a HeNe.

This is the highest-leverage current fact in the platform. A 2.5 W cw mid-IR source with sub-MHz-class stability is a step change over DFG. It makes saturation/double-resonance experiments, much weaker bands, and far more dilute species accessible. If you ask him one technical question, ask what the OPO now puts in reach that DFG did not. It is 2025 work, it is his own design, and almost no outside candidate will have noticed it.

3 · Buffer-Gas Cooling + Cavity-Enhanced Comb Spectroscopy (with the Ye group)

The method

  • Laser-desorb or oven-sublime a large molecule (C60) into a cryogenic cell flooded with cold helium.
  • Momentum-transfer collisions with He thermalize rotations and translations — down to 30 K in the 2025 work.
  • Probe with a mid-IR frequency comb locked to a high-finesse cavity (CE-DFCS): 10,000+ simultaneous "teeth", each a narrow probe, each amplified by thousands of cavity passes.

Why anyone cares

  • At 8.4 µm they resolved the R(J = 0–29) progression of 12C60 and saw certain transitions vanish entirely — a direct consequence of perfect icosahedral symmetry and the Bose statistics of 60 identical 12C nuclei.
  • The same platform showed rotational ergodicity breaking: icosahedral rovibrational fine structure, first predicted in 1986, observed for the first time in any physical system.
  • Turned sideways, the identical apparatus becomes a breathalyzer: four disease biomarkers detected simultaneously in exhaled breath, later extended to detecting SARS-CoV-2 infection.

4 · Single-Molecule FRET: Confocal + TIRF, with P and T as Knobs

Signal path

  • Label one RNA/DNA construct with a Cy3 donor and Cy5 acceptor; immobilize it on a passivated surface.
  • Excite the donor; energy transfer efficiency EFRET reports the donor–acceptor distance through the Förster R6 dependence.
  • Record EFRET(t) trajectories — high E = folded, low E = unfolded. Dwell-time histograms give kfold and kunfold directly, molecule by molecule.
  • Time-correlated single photon counting for photon-level timing; stroboscopic smFRET to beat camera blur and reach the frame-acquisition limit.

The Nesbitt twist: thermodynamics, not just pictures

  • Vary temperature → van 't Hoff / Eyring analysis → ΔH, ΔS, and activation barriers for a single tertiary contact.
  • Vary pressure → activation and reaction volumes ΔV; DNA hairpin hybridization under extreme pressure.
  • Vary both → the 2025 two-dimensional (P/T) study, which validates a Maxwell relation at the single-molecule level. That is a thermodynamic identity being tested on one molecule at a time.
  • Vary crowding → PEG crowders; RNA folded ~35× faster under cell-like crowding, and smaller crowders crowd better (depletion-force modeling).

What He Outsources — Your Opening

  • High-level electronic structure and PES fitting. He repeatedly brings in theorists — van der Avoird, Loreau, Kirk Peterson, Wes Allen, Changala, Tscherbul, Kłos/Kotochigova. The group measures; partners compute.
  • Large-amplitude-motion variational calculations. Cyclobutyl puckering, CH2OH torsion, ammonium-ion tunneling — all problems where a rigid-rotor model fails and you need a proper vibrational Hamiltonian on a computed surface.
  • Microwave/rotational precision. His resolution is spectacular for IR (tens of MHz) but rotational spectroscopy reaches kHz. Hyperfine structure, nuclear quadrupole coupling and precise ground-state constants are not his instrument's natural output.
  • Modern statistical and ML-based analysis of large spectral and single-molecule datasets — line-list assignment, trajectory state detection, automated fitting.
Strategic read: three of those four are things you can already do or are already doing. That is the argument — not "I admire your work," but "here are the calculations and the rotational-precision measurements your IR programme currently has to ask other people for."

The Core Insight

You are not applying to an adjacent field. You work on the exact molecular systems that built his career — Rg–HCl complexes, Rg2/Rg3–HX trimers, HCl–water clusters, N2–HCl — but with a different and complementary instrument. He mapped them with infrared lasers in slit jets; you measure them with microwave Fourier-transform spectroscopy. His technique gives vibrational dynamics and band origins at tens of MHz. Yours gives ground-state structure, hyperfine structure and nuclear quadrupole coupling at kHz. That is not overlap. That is the missing half.

The Gaps — Name Them Before He Does

Be straight about this. He is a NIST metrologist; he will spot a bluff instantly, and a candidate who maps their own gaps accurately reads as more capable, not less. Your real gaps are: (1) you have not run a discharge source or worked with transient open-shell radicals and molecular ions; (2) your optical experience is microwave/RF, not mid-IR nonlinear optics — OPOs, PPLN, cavity locking; (3) you have not done condensed-phase or single-molecule work at all; (4) your degree programme is Industrial & Systems Engineering, which he will ask about.
